# Question Answering Skill

## Learned Question-Answering Rules

- Return only the requested short answer. When the prompt asks for a short answer or says to return only the answer, output exactly the minimal entity or phrase that directly answers the question, with no explanation, no full sentences, and no extra punctuation unless the answer itself requires it. Do not add extra descriptive terms, full formal names, full titles, parentheses, dates, release years, or quotation marks when they are not part of the answer itself; but if the question directly supplies or implies an honorific/title (e.g., a clue stating the person was knighted, ennobled, ordained, or otherwise titled) and the matching context uses that honorific with the name, retain it exactly rather than stripping it. Preserve official designations, leading or internal articles that are part of a proper name, and leading generic feature classifiers such as Lake, Mount, Cape, Fort, or Bay exactly as the matching context uses them for the requested entity. Preserve a leading article when the context's canonical answer phrase for a definitional class includes it (e.g., a source line of the form "The X is ..."); otherwise do not add articles.
- For title/name questions, output the canonical title/name only. If a source shows a parenthetical release year after a title, omit the parenthetical year from the answer unless the year is part of the official title or explicitly requested.
- For personal-name answers, use the standard canonical given-name + surname form found in the context (e.g., "Stuart Sutcliffe", not "Stu Sutcliffe"). Do not default to the most expanded form with middle initials or expanded middle names unless the question explicitly requests the full formal name; if the context contains both a standard two-word personal name and a longer official variant with middle name/initial, prefer the standard two-word form for short-answer grading.
- When multiple forms of a person's name appear, prefer the formal given-name + surname form from introductory/title context over nicknames/diminutives; do not choose a shorter informal variant just because it is stylistically salient.
- For non-person descriptive clues that use "this" or "it" requesting a work/entity, return the exact shortest title or label found in the supplied context, not the full official title or expanded name. If context contains both a full title and a shorter form or abbreviation for the same work, favor the shorter form unless the full title or expansion is explicitly requested.
- If the prompt is a declarative clue, fact-like sentence, or cloze fragment, determine the requested slot and answer type from the wording and syntax. The target may be a person/entity, date, time period, location/facility, title, or descriptive phrase; return the context phrase that exactly fills that slot instead of defaulting to the most salient associated person/entity.
- Treat anaphoric/demonstrative phrases such as "this X", "that X", or "these X" as hard references: resolve them to their actual antecedent in the context and return that referent, not another X that only overlaps with the prompt's keywords.
- For "This ..." declarative clues, let the main subject of the context (repeated document titles/opening sentences and direct factual matches) determine the answer. Do not switch to another entity that appears in a single snippet merely because it matches a status, temporal, or superlative word in the question (e.g., a current title); weigh it against the main subject and the most specific factual predicates.
- Do not echo unrelated year/number/detail cues from the clue.
- Exception for fill-in-the-blank questions: when a blank is part of a multi-word term (e.g., "___ & X"), return the complete canonical term exactly as it appears in the context, including ampersands, capitalization, and suffixes, unless the question explicitly asks for only the missing portion.
- Identify the question focus and constraints: what entity, attribute, or relationship is requested (e.g., names, dates, aliases, slogans, units, superlatives, time, location).
- Identify key clue terms, quoted phrases, or pronouns such as "this X" or "it" in the question.
- Scan all supplied context snippets (e.g., [DOC]/[TLE]/[PAR]), especially document titles and first sentences, for exact or near-exact matches and paraphrases of those clues.
- Treat the question as a missing-entity clue; prefer snippets that use the same wording as the question or state the requested relationship directly (e.g., formerly known as, slogan, equal to). When a context passage mirrors the question phrasing, the answer is usually the adjacent completing text.
- Extract the exact short noun phrase or proper noun from the matching context; preserve the form used in the context, prefer the shortest standard term, and match the specificity level of the question. Avoid over-inferring beyond the context; a semantically correct but longer answer may still miss exact-match grading.
- Ignore snippets that mention only part of the question but do not support the answer.
- When no choices are given, use lexical overlap only as a first filter, not as the final ranking. Rank candidate passages by whether they supply the exact completion for the requested slot and whether their answer's category matches the question focus, even if they have lower overall keyword overlap.


- Use question punctuation and format cues as hard constraints before selecting from context. If the prompt ends with or contains a short code/abbreviation after a phrase or colon, treat that code as the requested target: output the full name or expansion found in context for that code, not another entity that is merely associated with the phrase.

- Treat a quoted fragment in the prompt as a fixed portion of a known context phrase, title, or name and infer the missing neighboring words from its position. A leading ellipsis (e.g., "...phrase") signals a missing prefix: locate the exact fragment in the context and output the adjacent preceding word(s) that complete the quotation. A trailing quoted suffix after a demonstrative or descriptive reference (e.g., a creator wrote about these/this Xs "of <tail>" or "<tail>") also signals a missing-prefix completion: find the context entity/title whose tail exactly matches the quoted fragment, and return only the preceding component that corresponds to the described head, omitting the quoted tail and the rest of the full title/name. Do not answer with the dominant full title, author, or performer merely because the fragment appears in that title.

- After locating the best passage by lexical overlap, re-read the question for relation words and format indicators such as ellipsis, colon, abbreviation, quotation marks, and named-for cues, and use them to filter candidate answers. Do not choose an answer only because it has the strongest lexical overlap or highest frequency in the context.

- When the context names one primary or typical source/example among several alternatives (e.g., "mainly", "especially", "particularly", "usually", "primarily"), return only that single answer. Do this even when the prompt uses plural wording such as "these animals", "these sources", or "these items", and even when a sentence pairs the primary source with another in a disjunctive list (e.g., "usually X or Y"). A focus cue in another authoritative passage, a repeated singular emphasis, or a direct "especially/particularly" gloss narrows the answer to the single primary source. Do not join alternatives with "or" or return a list unless the question explicitly asks for all alternatives.

- For terse one-word or entity-only prompts, do not output a longer descriptive phrase or repeat the prompt entity. Infer the hidden attribute from the context, and return only the value itself, not the role/title/label phrase around it. When the prompt names a person with an honorific/title and the context attaches a country/location, return the bare country/location rather than the full role/title phrase. When the prompt is a quoted creative-work title (optionally with a release year), return the principal credited person/creator directly stated in the context (e.g., director, author, artist), not a genre, medium, plot, or category descriptor. Only if no such specific attribution exists, fall back to the shortest adjacent field explicitly attached to that term (for a place, usually the state/province/country; for other entities, the category/type). Do not include generic role or category labels as substitutes for the hidden value.
- For 'this type/subtype/kind of X' questions, prefer the specific named type directly tied to the clue in the context over a generic modifier such as 'modern X' or just 'X'. A generic descriptor that also matches the clue is not sufficient when a concrete subtype is available in a matching snippet.
- If a context document contains a direct answer line for the clue (e.g., a game-show-style clue/answer line or pipe-delimited answer field), copy the answer exactly as written, including leading honorifics or titles and full multi-word names. Direct answer-line token matching outranks the general rule to shorten or strip titles.


- Before finalizing an answer, enumerate every concrete constraint in the question (amount/denomination, time/date range, habitat, behavior, symbol/abbreviation, etc.) and require the chosen candidate to satisfy all of them from the supplied context. A candidate with strong partial lexical overlap (e.g., matching only a shared abbreviation/symbol and broad category) must not beat a candidate that is supported for the full constraint set; when multiple candidates share an abbreviation or symbol, the remaining concrete constraints decide the answer.
- For a "This X" clue that describes a general class/category with class-level properties and the clue does not already supply the generic class/head noun in the slot, return the common class term that defines those properties as used and introduced/defined in the context, including a leading definite article if the source introduces or defines the class that way. Do not substitute a more specific member/subtype merely because that member is frequent or has higher keyword overlap; reserve specific members/subtypes for questions that explicitly ask for a type/subtype/kind or whose constraints match only that member.


- For list-of-members or completion prompts that name same-type items and ask for the missing one, do not mimic a bare list form when the context's canonical form for the missing member includes a leading generic classifier or official designation. Return the full named entity as written in the matching context, because the prompt may omit generic words for brevity.
- When the context distinguishes between a shortened base name and an official or full designation, choose the designation that exactly satisfies the factual clue. Do not strip an official title or distinguishing prefix only to produce the shortest shared base name.


- When a declarative or appositive clue already supplies a generic category/head noun in the slot (e.g., "this vitamin", "this source", "this state university"), do not answer with the full common-noun phrase that includes that category/head noun. Determine which tokens the question itself already supplies or implies, and return only the remaining distinguishing classifier, modifier, or designation from the matching context phrase (for single-letter or symbolic designations such as a vitamin letter, chemical symbol, or class code, return the symbol alone unless the full name is explicitly requested). Before emitting a full noun phrase, test whether the shorter modifier/designation alone still fits the clue syntactically and matches the context; a full phrase may be semantically correct but will still miss exact-match grading.
- For clues that identify a parent entity by a specific subunit and then contrast it with another subunit (e.g., "this [institution/place/group] at X, not Y"), return the shared parent/system name from the context without the specific subunit qualifier. Preserve the canonical parent form as written, including a leading article if the parent name carries one.


- For terse prompts that list two or more named entities with '&', 'and', or commas but no explicit relation, do not answer with another entity associated with only one of the listed names (such as a parent, spouse, creator, product, or member). Treat the listed names as the candidate set; identify the shared attribute, class, location, organization, or relationship that the context attributes to all of them, and return that shared field exactly as used in the context. Do not emit a related person or other co-occurring entity just because it appears frequently with one of the listed names.


- For terse prompts made of a category/type word plus a named subject (e.g., "Rock group X", "Novel Y", "Act Z"), do not copy the full descriptive label or category phrase attached to that subject from the context. Identify the hidden slot implied by the category/type word, then return only the value of that slot. In tribute/cover-band contexts, the hidden value is the original group or artist being covered/modeled, not the words "tribute band" or "cover band"; if the context labels something as "X Tribute Band" or "X cover band", return the named original act X.


- Match the grammatical number of a common-noun answer to the canonical form used in the supplied context. When the question asks for the fruit, produce, commodity, item, or category featured by an event, competition, festival, location, or other label, and the context establishes that item in the plural (e.g., in a heading, list, count-noun phrase, or description of the event's focus), return the plural form even if the prompt uses a singular class/head noun such as "this fruit" or "this item". Conversely, if the context defines or names the requested term as a singular count noun or uncountable/mass noun, return that exact singular or mass form. Do not singularize or pluralize the answer solely to match the surface number of the prompt, and do not derive the answer from the singular modifier in a title if the context's canonical noun is plural.

- For terse prompts consisting of a locative preposition plus a list of place names (e.g., "In X & Y", "At X and Y"), do not return a containing or adjacent place such as a country, state, region, or city. The places already named in the prompt are the clue, not the requested answer. Infer and return the non-geographic shared attribute that the context attributes to those places (often a language or demonym) in the bare singular form attested by the context. Return a containing location only when the prompt explicitly asks for location/containership (e.g., "X and Y are in which country?").
- For terse declarative or "this" clues that identify a creative work through its premise, format, participants, or classification rather than through an explicit work/entity noun, treat the requested slot as the short common title actually used in body text or quoted references. If the supplied context contains both a shorter common title and a longer full official title/subtitle, often appearing in document title lines, return the shorter title exactly as used in the matching factual passage. Do not expand the short title to the full official title merely because document headings repeat the full form.
- For a terse one-word or entity-only prompt that names a bare place, infer the hidden attribute from the leading factual sentence and return only that value. When the context attributes a country, state/province, region, or other administrative location to the place, return that containing location directly. Do not echo the prompt entity and do not output the place's full official, legal, translated, or alternate name from the context unless the question explicitly asks for the official name/form.
- For location/facility clues that state an event happened at a described place, treat document titles and headings as weak evidence. A title such as a military plant/museum page can have high lexical overlap with the clue's category and location words while its body text places the requested event at a different site or process step. Do not choose a title candidate unless a body sentence connects the requested event/time to that named facility. If the context distinguishes related sites or steps (construction at one site, final assembly/launch/landing at another), match the question's verb and time range to the corresponding site. Return the named place actually tied to the event in the body text, even if that passage has lower keyword overlap with the title.
