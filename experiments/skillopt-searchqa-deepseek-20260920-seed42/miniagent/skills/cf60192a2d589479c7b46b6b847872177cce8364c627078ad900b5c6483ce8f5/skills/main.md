# Question Answering Skill

## Learned Question-Answering Rules

- Return only the requested short answer. When the prompt asks for a short answer or says to return only the answer, output exactly the minimal entity or phrase that directly answers the question, with no explanation, no full sentences, and no extra punctuation unless the answer itself requires it. Do not add extra descriptive terms, full formal names, full titles, articles, parentheses, dates, release years, or quotation marks unless the question explicitly asks for those forms.
- For title/name questions, output the canonical title/name only. If a source shows a parenthetical release year after a title, omit the parenthetical year from the answer unless the year is part of the official title or explicitly requested.
- For personal-name answers, use the standard canonical given-name + surname form found in the context (e.g., "Stuart Sutcliffe", not "Stu Sutcliffe"). Do not default to the most expanded form with middle initials or expanded middle names unless the question explicitly requests the full formal name; if the context contains both a standard two-word personal name and a longer official variant with middle name/initial, prefer the standard two-word form for short-answer grading.
- When multiple forms of a person's name appear, prefer the formal given-name + surname form from introductory/title context over nicknames/diminutives; do not choose a shorter informal variant just because it is stylistically salient.
- For non-person descriptive clues that use "this" or "it" requesting a work/entity, return the exact shortest title or label found in the supplied context, not the full official title or expanded name. If context contains both a full title and a shorter form or abbreviation for the same work, favor the shorter form unless the full title or expansion is explicitly requested.
- If the prompt is a declarative clue, fact-like sentence, or cloze fragment, determine the requested slot and answer type from the wording and syntax. The target may be a person/entity, date, time period, location/facility, title, or descriptive phrase; return the context phrase that exactly fills that slot instead of defaulting to the most salient associated person/entity.
- Treat anaphoric/demonstrative phrases such as "this X", "that X", or "these X" as hard references: resolve them to their actual antecedent in the context and return that referent, not another X that only overlaps with the prompt's keywords.
- For "This ..." declarative clues, let the main subject of the context (repeated document titles/opening sentences and direct factual matches) determine the answer. Do not switch to another entity that appears in a single snippet merely because it matches a status, temporal, or superlative word in the question (e.g., a current title); weigh it against the main subject and the most specific factual predicates.
- Do not echo unrelated year/number/detail cues from the clue.
- Exception for fill-in-the-blank questions: when a blank is part of a multi-word term (e.g., "___ & X"), return the complete canonical term exactly as it appears in the context, including ampersands, capitalization, and suffixes, unless the question explicitly asks for only the missing portion. If the prompt supplies the surrounding words of a longer canonical phrase and asks to fill the blank, return only the missing token(s) exactly as they appear in the matching context phrase; return the full phrase only when the prompt asks for the full canonical term or does not already provide the surrounding words.
- Identify the question focus and constraints: what entity, attribute, or relationship is requested (e.g., names, dates, aliases, slogans, units, superlatives, time, location).
- Identify key clue terms, quoted phrases, or pronouns such as "this X" or "it" in the question.
- Scan all supplied context snippets (e.g., [DOC]/[TLE]/[PAR]), especially document titles and first sentences, for exact or near-exact matches and paraphrases of those clues.
- Treat the question as a missing-entity clue; prefer snippets that use the same wording as the question or state the requested relationship directly (e.g., formerly known as, slogan, equal to). When a context passage mirrors the question phrasing, the answer is usually the adjacent completing text.
- Extract the exact short noun phrase or proper noun from the matching context; preserve the form used in the context, prefer the shortest standard term, and match the specificity level of the question. Avoid over-inferring beyond the context; a semantically correct but longer answer may still miss exact-match grading.
- Ignore snippets that mention only part of the question but do not support the answer.
- When no choices are given, use lexical overlap only as a first filter, not as the final ranking. Rank candidate passages by whether they supply the exact completion for the requested slot and whether their answer's category matches the question focus, even if they have lower overall keyword overlap.


- Use question punctuation and format cues as hard constraints before selecting from context. If the prompt ends with or contains a short code/abbreviation after a phrase or colon, treat that code as the requested target: output the full name or expansion found in context for that code, not another entity that is merely associated with the phrase.

- Treat a quoted fragment with a leading ellipsis (e.g., "...phrase") as a missing-prefix fill-in-the-blank. Locate the exact fragment in the context and output the adjacent preceding word(s) that complete the quotation; do not answer with the dominant title, author, or performer merely because the fragment appears in that title.

- After locating the best passage by lexical overlap, re-read the question for relation words and format indicators such as ellipsis, colon, abbreviation, quotation marks, and named-for cues, and use them to filter candidate answers. Do not choose an answer only because it has the strongest lexical overlap or highest frequency in the context.

- When the context names one primary or typical source/example among several alternatives (e.g., "mainly", "especially", "particularly", "usually"), return only that primary single answer rather than joining alternatives with "or", unless the question explicitly asks for all alternatives.

- For terse one-word or entity-only prompts, do not output a longer descriptive phrase or repeat the prompt entity. Infer the hidden attribute from the context and return only the shortest adjacent field explicitly attached to that term (for a place, usually the state/province/country; for other entities, the category/type).
- For 'this type/subtype/kind of X' questions, prefer the specific named type directly tied to the clue in the context over a generic modifier such as 'modern X' or just 'X'. A generic descriptor that also matches the clue is not sufficient when a concrete subtype is available in a matching snippet.
- If a context document contains a direct answer line for the clue (e.g., a game-show-style clue/answer line or pipe-delimited answer field), copy the answer exactly as written, including leading honorifics or titles, full multi-word names, leading articles/determiners such as "a", "an", or "the", and parenthetical first-name forms (e.g., a surname preceded by a parenthetical given name/nickname). Preserve parentheses, capitalization, and any honorifics/titles exactly. Direct answer-line token matching outranks the general rule to shorten or strip titles, articles, or parentheticals.


- For closed-book QA with supplied context, do not issue shell/web/tool calls to verify or search for candidates. After scanning the context, commit to the best-supported entity from the context and return it as the final short answer. If no single snippet contains the full clue wording, resolve the remaining pronoun/role against the candidate that best matches the rest of the clue, and still output a short answer rather than an empty or tool-only response.
- For definitional/classification clues (e.g., "this is the term for ..." or "what is the term for ..."), preserve the exact grammatical form of the defining context phrase, including articles and singular/plural number. If the context defines something as "a/an X" or the question asks for a singular term, return that exact singular noun phrase with the required article; do not convert to a bare plural or drop a required article. Similarly, when a declarative clue or fill-in-the-blank uses grammatical number cues, match the expected slot's number; do not singularize just to give a shorter or more generic term.
- For personal-name answers, prefer the exact name form attested in the supplied context. If only a surname or mononym appears in the context (e.g., in a document title, identifier, or passage), return that bare form with normal capitalization; do not expand it to a full given-name + surname not present in the context, even if world knowledge supplies one. The preference for a canonical given-name + surname applies only when that full form is actually present in the context.
- Treat a short label in a document title or identifier that directly names the clue's subject as a direct answer label: copy that label exactly, without expanding or formalizing it, unless the question explicitly asks for a fuller form.
- When the prompt introduces a foreign/local term with an indefinite article or apposition (e.g., "This, an X in Y..."), return the term's explicit definition, translation, or common-noun category from the context. Preserve the indefinite article when the prompt uses "an/a" before that term. Do not replace it with a specific proper name, official title, or emblem that merely contains the term, even if that proper name matches other clue words.
- For bare comma-separated prompts listing same-type entities, treat the listed items as the candidate set and return the single listed entity that satisfies the implied context relationship or distinction. Do not output the broader group or category name containing those entities unless the prompt explicitly asks for it; if the prompt explicitly asks for their shared category/species/type/class, return that shared field or class noun exactly as used in the context, not one of the listed entities or a merely co-occurring name.
- For title/work-name answers, use the canonical compact orthography if the target is standardly written as one word or with a specific spacing/hyphenation, even if some context snippets render it as a spaced descriptive phrase. Before finalizing, check whether the expected answer token should be a compound or spaced form and match the reference orthography rather than automatically copying the most frequent surface form.


- For declarative clues beginning with a pronoun or possessive determiner (e.g., "After he...", "During his...", "He...", "Its area equals...", "Its capital is..."), resolve the pronoun to the antecedent/owner entity established by the context (document titles, opening sentences, full-name introductions, or the entity to which the property is attributed), not to the associated work, achievement, spouse, detail, or property value named later in the sentence.
- When the clue contains a prepositional placeholder or circumlocution such as "the X of this", "in this X", "for this X", or "the [modifier] type/variety/kind of this", treat the prepositional slot as the missing answer. Find the full matching phrase in the context and return the completing noun phrase as the question slot requires; specifically, for a "the [modifier] type/variety/kind of this" circumlocution, return only the base noun from the matching [Modifier] [Base Noun] phrase and omit leading modifiers unless the full phrase is explicitly requested.
- For etymology/definition clues framed as "From X, meaning Y, it's the name/named..." or clues asking for "this term/phrase/word" that supply a definition, etymology, or description, isolate the word or term being named/defined. Return that term exactly as it appears in the applicable context (preserving lower-case common-noun form where appropriate), not the source word, root, foreign-language etymon, gloss, or expanded definition.
- Treat a quoted fragment that ends at a boundary and is followed by a request for what comes next (e.g., "from X to this Y" or "to what Y") as a missing-suffix fill-in-the-blank. Locate the exact fragment in the supplied context and return the adjacent completing word or phrase that immediately follows it; do not answer with the title, performer, or entity merely because the fragment appears in it.
- For clues of the form "X and one of these" or "X needs/wants Y and one of these", treat "one of these" as a missing item from a described set. Locate the context passage that names the known element(s) and lists the companion element(s), then return the missing category or term exactly as used in that list. Do not output a longer descriptive phrase unless the question asks for the full list.
- For clues pairing a quoted work title or known entity/event with "this [role/man/person/description]", resolve "this" to the entity that matches both the quoted/known item and the requested relationship in context. Return that entity's canonical short name, not another associated person, work, or more salient but non-co-occurring entity.
- For locative possessive clues such as "this city's [team/entity]", "this state's [X]", or "this country's [X]", return the location name that modifies/owns the entity in the context, not the entity name.
- When a clue contains wordplay or punning around the requested entity, ignore the pun as a distractor. Determine the syntactic requested head noun (e.g., author, work, person, plant) and use factual context matches for that entity rather than the punning phrase or overlapping pun terms.
